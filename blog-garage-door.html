Your garage door is one of the most-used entry points in your home. Regular maintenance prevents problems and extends its lifespan.

Common Issues & Fixes

Door Won't Open

Check Remote Batteries: The simplest solution first—replace the batteries in your remote control.

Check Power Source: Ensure the opener is plugged in and the circuit breaker hasn't tripped.

Check Manual Lock: Make sure you haven't accidentally engaged the manual lock on the inside of the door.

Disconnect Switch: Verify the wall disconnect switch hasn't been accidentally turned off.

Noisy Operation

Excessive noise usually indicates parts need lubrication or replacement. Apply garage door lubricant to:

  • Hinges: All pivot points between door sections
  • Rollers: The wheels that guide the door up and down (avoid lubricating nylon rollers)
  • Tracks: Light coating on inside of tracks
  • Springs: Lightly coat torsion springs (if accessible)
  • Opener chain/belt: Follow manufacturer recommendations

Use silicone-based or lithium-based garage door lubricant—not WD-40, which attracts dirt.

Door Reverses Before Closing

Adjust Force Settings: Your opener has close force adjustment. If set too sensitively, it will reverse at slight resistance. Increase the close force setting slightly (refer to owner's manual).

Clean Photo Eyes: Safety sensors on either side of the door detect obstructions. Wipe them clean with a soft cloth. Ensure they're aligned—both should have solid indicator lights.

Check for Obstructions: Look for anything blocking the door's path or interfering with the tracks.

Uneven Closing

Spring Tension: If one side closes faster than the other, the springs may have uneven tension. This is dangerous to adjust yourself—call a professional for spring work.

Track Alignment: Check that tracks on both sides are equally spaced from the door and properly aligned vertically.

Remote Not Working

Reprogram Remote: Follow your opener's manual to reprogram the remote. Usually involves pressing a "learn" button on the opener and then the remote button.

Replace Battery: If reprogramming doesn't work, replace the remote battery.

Check Range: Move closer to the door. If it works up close but not from afar, the antenna on the opener may need adjustment.

Maintenance Schedule

Monthly Tasks

Visual Inspection: Look for worn cables, frayed wires, loose hardware, or damaged rollers. Address issues promptly before they worsen.

Test Auto-Reverse Feature: Place a 2x4 board flat on the ground where the door would close. The door should reverse when it contacts the board. If it doesn't, adjust sensitivity or call a professional—this is a critical safety feature.

Quarterly Tasks (Every 3 Months)

Lubricate Moving Parts: Apply garage door lubricant to all moving parts as described above. Wipe away excess to prevent dirt buildup.

Clean Tracks: Use a damp cloth to remove dirt and debris from inside the tracks. Don't use harsh cleaners that could damage protective coatings.

Tighten Hardware: Use a socket wrench to tighten all roller brackets and bolts. The vibration from daily use can loosen hardware over time.

Annual Tasks

Professional Inspection: Have a qualified technician inspect your entire system. They can spot issues you might miss and perform tasks that require special tools or expertise.

Balance Testing: Disconnect the opener and manually lift the door halfway. It should stay in place—if it falls or rises, the springs need adjustment (professional task).

Spring Adjustment: Springs lose tension over time. A professional can adjust or replace springs to maintain proper door balance.

⚠️ Safety Warning: Spring Replacement

NEVER attempt to repair or replace garage door springs yourself.

Garage door springs are under extreme tension (150-200 pounds of force or more). If a spring breaks or slips during DIY repair, it can cause:

  • Serious injury or death
  • Severe lacerations
  • Broken bones
  • Property damage

Always hire a professional for spring work. They have specialized tools, training, and insurance to safely handle this dangerous task.

When to Call a Professional

  • Spring replacement or adjustment
  • Cable repair or replacement
  • Door off track
  • Opener motor failure
  • Bent or damaged tracks
  • Annual comprehensive inspection
  • Any issue you're uncomfortable handling

Extending Your Garage Door's Lifespan

Average Lifespan: 15-30 years with proper maintenance

Ways to Maximize Life:

  • Follow the maintenance schedule consistently
  • Address small issues before they become major problems
  • Keep tracks clean and aligned
  • Don't ignore unusual noises—they're warning signs
  • Test safety features monthly
  • Hire professionals for technical repairs

Key Takeaways

  • Test auto-reverse safety feature monthly with a 2x4 board
  • Lubricate all moving parts quarterly with garage door lubricant
  • Tighten hardware quarterly to prevent loosening from vibration
  • Clean photo eye sensors if door reverses unexpectedly
  • Adjust close force settings if door is too sensitive
  • NEVER attempt spring repairs yourself—always hire professionals
  • Annual professional inspection catches problems early
  • Balance door manually once per year to check spring tension
  • Average garage door lifespan: 15-30 years with maintenance
